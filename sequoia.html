Sequoia

Packaging for a biotech that would rather talk about absorption than ingredients.

Client
Sequoia
Year
2025
Scope
Kit sleeves, single-product carton, print artwork
Market
Israel — Hebrew and English

Sequoia builds liposomal delivery systems. Their whole argument is that most supplements never actually reach the cell.

That gave the packaging its job. Not to shout about ingredients, which is what the shelf around it already does, but to look like something engineered — closer to a lab than a health-food aisle.

So: one stock, one typeface, one accent colour per product, and a lot of empty space. The name of the kit is the largest thing on the box, and everything else waits its turn.

A system, not five designs

Five kits share a single sleeve die. Only three things change between them — the name, the Hebrew line beneath it, and one accent colour carried by a small icon. Lipo For Me runs coral with a flame; For Him blue with the male glyph; For Her salmon; For Us both glyphs interlocked; For Life violet with sparks.

The product list sits on the top face, so the contents read from above when the boxes are stacked on a pharmacy shelf.

The name of the kit is the largest thing on the box. Everything else waits its turn.

The reverse

The back carries the wordmark, the line “Delivering Precision, Empowering Wellness”, and the barcode — nothing else. It is laid out inverted in the die, so it reads the right way up when you flip the box towards you rather than spinning it.

Single carton

The individual product box runs the same stock and type at a quarter of the size. The front makes the absorption claim as a chart rather than a sentence; the side panel carries five icons for the things the buyer actually wants confirmed — developed with Technion scientists, lab-tested, researched and manufactured in Israel.

The inside face is printed too, so opening it is not a dead end.

Flat

The sleeve unrolled: glue flap, back, side, front, side. 298 × 171 × 74 mm folded.
