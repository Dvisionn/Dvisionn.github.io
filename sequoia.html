Sequoia

Packaging for a biotech that would rather talk about absorption than ingredients.

Client
Sequoia
Year
2025
Scope
Kit range, single-product carton, campaign, print artwork
Languages
Hebrew and English

Make-A-Wish

A campaign run as a collaboration between Sequoia and Make-A-Wish, the children's charity. Products packed into a printed tote with a card, sold as one set.

The wordmark runs the full height of the bag rather than sitting politely in the middle of it — the one place in the system where the type gets to be loud.

Sequoia builds liposomal delivery systems. Their whole argument is that most supplements never actually reach the cell.

That gave the packaging its job. Not to shout about ingredients, which is what the shelf around it already does, but to look like something engineered — closer to a lab than a health-food aisle.

So: one stock, one typeface, one accent colour per product, and a lot of empty space. The name of the kit is the largest thing on the box, and everything else waits its turn.

Five kits, one shelf

Each kit bundles a combination of supplements aimed at one thing — metabolism, men's energy, women's hormones, fertility, pregnancy. Five different people are buying these, and they sit next to each other in the same pharmacy.

So the range had to separate without splitting. The die is identical across all five. What changes is the name, the Hebrew line beneath it, and a single accent colour carried by one small icon: coral and a flame, blue and the male glyph, salmon and the female, the two interlocked, violet and a pair of sparks.

The product list sits on the top face, so the contents read from above when the boxes are stacked.

The name of the kit is the largest thing on the box. Everything else waits its turn.

The reverse

The back carries the wordmark, the line “Delivering Precision, Empowering Wellness”, and the barcode — nothing else. It is laid out inverted in the die, so it reads the right way up when you flip the box towards you rather than spinning it.

Single carton

The individual product box runs the same stock and type at a quarter of the size. The front makes the absorption claim as a chart rather than a sentence; the side panel carries five icons for the things the buyer actually wants confirmed — developed by research scientists, lab-tested, and made under GMP conditions.

The inside face is printed too, so opening it is not a dead end.

In use

The packaging in the client's own social posts — unboxings, shelf shots and campaign films.
